# Weekly Market Letter
## Week of 09-13 November 2026

## 1. What Moved Markets This Week

1. **European equities rallied sharply, lifting the Stoxx Europe 600 and DAX to record highs.** Supported by gains in technology shares and corporate earnings reports, European benchmark indices advanced throughout the week, with the Stoxx Europe 600 achieving three consecutive record highs. Easing market concerns over artificial intelligence demand provided broad support across continental exchanges. However, equity indices pulled back from their weekly peaks in late trading as leadership uncertainty at Nvidia and shifting central bank interest rate expectations weighed on sentiment.

2. **US Treasury yields surged as persistent inflation readings and hawkish Federal Reserve commentary heightened rate concerns.** Short-dated debt led the sell-off, with the US 2-year Treasury yield rising 37.0 basis points to 4.88% while the 10-year yield climbed 29.0 basis points to 5.15%. Headlines pointed to hotter-than-expected inflation data and statements from Fed officials as the primary drivers of the move. The sharp adjustment in rate expectations pushed the Dollar Index to a 52-week high, while driving gold and EUR/USD down to 52-week lows.

3. **Leadership shake-ups at Nvidia triggered a sharp divergence within mega-cap technology equities.** Reports indicating that Chief Executive Officer Jensen Huang resigned sent Nvidia shares down 12.75%, pushing the stock to a 52-week low. Market headlines noted that the executive changes directed investor focus toward alternative chip designers, lifting Alphabet by 3.86% as Google's custom AI architecture drew attention. Meta also gained 5.17% over the week despite broader weakness in US rate-sensitive sectors.

Investors will monitor whether forward guidance can stabilize tech sentiment, which directly impacts European semiconductor suppliers such as ASML and broader index momentum.

Because US yields rose significantly faster than euro area AAA yields (+37.0 bps on US 2Y versus +12.6 bps on Euro 10Y), euro stability will depend on domestic economic data confirming policy resilience relative to the Federal Reserve.

4. **European equity indices face technical tests at 52-week high extremes.** The Stoxx Europe 600 and DAX closed at 52-week highs, while the FTSE 100 sits near the top of its 52-week range. With US retail sales and central bank minutes on the immediate horizon, any unexpected macroeconomic weakness could prompt profit-taking across European large-caps, particularly given the divergence between rising sovereign bond yields and elevated equity market valuations.
