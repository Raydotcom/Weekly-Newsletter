# Weekly Market Letter
## Week of 27-31 July 2026

## 1. What Moved Markets This Week

1. **European equities rose as strong bank earnings and reduced Middle East tensions lifted risk sentiment across regional benchmarks.** Reports pointed to a pause in Middle East conflict concerns and easing oil prices, providing immediate support to European equity indexes. Earnings updates reinforced this momentum, with European lenders including Deutsche Bank, UBS, and Societe Generale posting trading-driven profit increases that extended the sector's two-year rally. Consequently, the DAX rose 2.11% and the CAC 40 gained 1.64% over the week.

2. **The Federal Reserve held policy rates steady, triggering volatility in US markets while European rate expectations adjusted to geopolitical risks.** US equity indexes saw sharp fluctuations and a midweek pullback after the Fed held rates unchanged, though indexes ultimately closed higher on the week. In Europe, market expectations shifted toward a potential September ECB rate hike path shaped by Middle East uncertainties. However, primary findings from the ECB indicated that eurozone spending reductions were being driven by geopolitical uncertainty rather than rising domestic inflation pressures.

3. **Divergent Big Tech earnings highlighted growing investor scrutiny over AI capital expenditure, driving sharp dispersion across global tech stocks.** Results across major US technology firms split market sentiment as investors demanded clearer evidence of returns on massive AI investments. Strong cloud revenue growth propelled Microsoft (+21.75%) and Amazon (+17.00%) significantly higher, while Meta (-6.47%) fell on capex concerns. In Europe, semiconductor equipment supplier ASML dropped 8.13% as capital spending reassessments weighed on chip supply chain vendors.

## 3. Why It Matters for Next Week

1. **Upcoming US labor market data and ISM manufacturing reports will test Federal Reserve policy assumptions.** With scheduled releases for US ISM readings and the monthly nonfarm payrolls report, investors will look for confirmation of cooling economic activity. The recent divergence in US Treasuries—where the 2-year yield fell 5.0 bps while the 10-year yield rose 6.0 bps—leaves short-end yields sensitive to labor data; weak figures would reinforce expectations for policy easing, whereas strong numbers could reverse recent front-end yield declines.

2. **Upcoming semiconductor earnings from AMD will show if hardware supplier pressures persist.** Following ASML's 8.13% drop this week, scheduled quarterly results from AMD will provide a critical test for chipmakers and capital equipment providers. Clear evidence of sustained hardware demand and AI capex translation would help stabilize European semiconductor suppliers, whereas weaker guidance risks deepening the retreat in high-valuation tech names.

3. **European banking sector valuations face heightened pressure at multi-year range extremes.** The Euro Stoxx Banks index and BNP Paribas both closed at 100% of their 52-week ranges following record quarterly profit reports. With valuations at the top of their range, further sector outperformance will depend on whether trading revenues and structural platform shifts can offset potential headwinds from future central bank rate cuts.

4. **ECB medium-term wage projections signal easing pressures, but near-term decisions remain tied to energy risks.** Primary data from the ECB wage tracker projects negotiated wage growth at 2.7% for Q1 2027, confirming that medium-term wage dynamics are stabilizing near target. However, policy trajectory through September remains constrained by near-term geopolitical developments in the Strait of Hormuz and their potential impact on European energy import prices.
