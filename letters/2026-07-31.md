# Weekly Market Letter
## Week of 27-31 July 2026

## 1. What Moved Markets This Week

1. **Easing Middle East tensions and falling energy prices boosted European equities.** Regional equity benchmarks rose firmly across the continent, led by a 2.11% gain in the DAX and a 1.64% gain in the CAC 40. Reporting pointed to a pause in Middle East conflict and easing US-Iran tensions as the main drivers behind a 6.88% drop in Brent crude prices to $90.12 per barrel. Lower energy input costs helped relieve margin pressures for European corporates, lifting broader equity sentiment throughout the week.

2. **European banking earnings extended sector leadership with record profitability.** Second-quarter earnings announcements reinforced a multi-year bull run for European lenders, with Deutsche Bank, UBS, and Societe Generale reporting profit jumps driven by equities trading and investment banking activity. The Euro Stoxx Banks index gained 2.05% to reach a 52-week high, while BNP Paribas rose 2.76% to hit its own 52-week high. Industry reports highlighted that robust underlying profitability and structural shifts in risk platforms continue to offset softer fixed-income trading volumes.

3. **Re-acceleration of Eurozone inflation fueled expectations for monetary tightening.** Headlines indicated that Eurozone inflation rose in July, leading market participants to scale up expectations for an European Central Bank interest rate increase in September. Although primary ECB tracking data showed negotiated wage growth stabilizing at 2.7% for Q1 2027, reports suggested that the ECB's rate path remains heavily influenced by geopolitical uncertainties and elevated headline price risks. The euro reacted positively to the hawkish shift, gaining 1.29% against the US dollar.

## 3. Why It Matters for Next Week

1. **ECB policy trajectory faces confirmation from incoming economic activity data.** Following reports of rising Eurozone inflation in July, markets have re-priced the probability of a September ECB rate increase. Scheduled economic coverage for the August 3–7 window will test whether July's revival in euro area services activity is sustained. Confirmation of sticky service-sector prices would cement expectations for policy tightening, whereas softer activity data would challenge the hawkish repricing seen in sovereign bond yields.

2. **Broadening tech earnings will test semiconductor momentum.** With the market digesting sharp divergence in mega-cap technology performance—highlighted by large gains in Microsoft (+21.75%) and Amazon (+17.00%) against weakness in Meta (-6.47%)—focus shifts to upcoming earnings from chipmakers and memory giants scheduled for August 3–7. Investors will watch whether hardware suppliers can validate the elevated AI expenditure plans of US hyperscalers, which will directly impact European semiconductor equipment sentiment after ASML's 7.76% drop this week.

3. **Valuations at 52-week range extremes leave banking equities exposed to sentiment shifts.** The Euro Stoxx Banks index and BNP Paribas ended the week at their 52-week highs (range position of 100), supported by strong quarterly profit reports. Because no explicit macroeconomic policy catalysts for European financials are slated for the immediate calendar, maintaining these levels will depend on whether sovereign bond yields remain stable. Any sudden re-widening of sovereign spreads from current tight levels (0.41%) could trigger short-term profit-taking in top-performing bank names.

4. **Energy market stability hinges on Strait of Hormuz developments.** The 6.88% fall in Brent crude was the primary driver of the European equity rally. Headlines confirm that talks regarding the Strait of Hormuz remain central to market positioning. If geopolitical negotiations prevent further disruptions, sustained lower energy costs will continue to support European industrial margin expansion; conversely, any breakdown in discussions would quickly re-introduce energy-driven inflation risks into European yield curves.
