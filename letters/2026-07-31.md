# Weekly Market Letter
## Week of 27-31 July 2026

## 1. What Moved Markets This Week

1. **Easing Middle East geopolitical risks and falling energy prices provided a broad tailwind for European equities.** Headlines indicated a pause in US-Iran conflict and focused on talks surrounding the Strait of Hormuz, driving Brent crude down 6.88% on the week. This reduction in energy cost pressures supported European sentiment, with news reports noting record levels set by both the DAX (+2.11%) and the CAC 40 (+1.64%).

2. **Strong corporate earnings in the European banking sector extended a multi-year rally, pushing financial indices to 52-week highs.** Quarterly results from Deutsche Bank, UBS, and Societe Generale highlighted trading-led profit jumps, reinforcing sector outperformance. Euro Stoxx Banks rose 2.05% to close at its 52-week high, supported by gains in constituent names such as BNP Paribas (+2.76%), which also reached a 52-week high.

3. **Accelerating Eurozone July inflation heightened ECB rate hike expectations, while the Federal Reserve maintained interest rates.** Headlines reported rising Eurozone inflation figures, prompting market participants and institutional forecasters to scale up expectations for an ECB rate increase in September. This occurred despite primary ECB communication showing the wage tracker projecting 2.7% for Q1 2027, indicating stable long-term negotiated wage pressures. In the US, the Federal Reserve held rates unchanged, triggering mid-week market volatility before equity indexes recovered on technology earnings.

## 3. Why It Matters for Next Week

1. **ECB policy expectations following July inflation figures will put Eurozone fixed income spreads under scrutiny.** With headline reporting highlighting rising July Eurozone inflation and Nomura pointing toward an ECB September rate hike path shaped by geopolitical and inflation developments, market participants will watch upcoming economic releases closely. Although the ECB wage tracker projects negotiated wage growth moderating to 2.7% in Q1 2027, near-term inflation readings could keep upward pressure on core yields (Euro area AAA 10Y at 3.23%). Any widening of the sovereign spread beyond 0.41% would signal fresh repricing of peripheral debt risks.

2. **Extremely high valuations in European banking equities require sustained fundamental confirmation.** The Euro Stoxx Banks index and individual titles like BNP Paribas closed at their 52-week highs (100% range position) on the back of Q2 trading revenue surges. For this sector momentum to continue next week, financial markets will need evidence that dealmaking and investment banking activity can offset any potential slowdown in fixed income trading noted by bank executives.

3. **Divergent short and long-end US Treasury moves signal shifting market focus toward economic growth and fiscal supply.** The divergence between US 2-year yields (-5.0 bps to 4.28%) and US 10-year yields (+6.0 bps to 4.75%) reflects a market re-evaluating the Fed's stance after holding policy rates steady. Confirmation of whether this curve behavior continues will depend on upcoming US economic data releases covering GDP and inflation, which will dictate whether front-end yields continue to fall relative to the long end.

4. **Crude oil's positioning at the midpoint of its 52-week range leaves European equity momentum dependent on Middle East diplomacy.** Brent crude's drop to $90.12 per barrel (52% range position) provided the primary relief catalyst for European record stock highs this week. Because news feeds cite Hormuz talks and US-Iran tension pauses as the key drivers, any reversal in geopolitical headlines next week would directly threaten the margin relief currently priced into broad European stock indexes.
