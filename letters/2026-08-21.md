# Weekly Market Letter
## Week of 17-21 August 2026

## What Moved Markets This Week

1. **Middle East war headlines and Iran sanction concerns drove energy prices higher and elevated European sovereign yields.** News reporting highlighted growing inflation risks linked to conflict in the Middle East, pushing Brent crude up 6.63% over the week while Euro zone bond yields neared 15-year highs. European equities faced persistent pressure under these inflation fears, leading the Stoxx Europe 600 down 0.56% and the CAC 40 down 1.76%. Although ECB communications noted moderate wage growth and remote stagnation risks, headlines indicated that traders are increasingly bracing for a more hawkish central bank response to energy-driven pricing pressures.

2. **Global mega-cap technology and semiconductor equities pulled back under AI capital expenditure and valuation scrutiny.** Reports citing European Central Bank research warned of correction risks surrounding the artificial intelligence boom, alongside growing scrutiny over off-balance-sheet debt tied to corporate AI spending. Ahead of Nvidia's Q2 earnings release, elevated market expectations weighed on sentiment across the sector. The selling pressure dragged the Nasdaq 100 down 2.45% and Nvidia down 4.64%, while European semiconductor leader ASML fell 4.66%.

3. **Broad US dollar weakness lifted precious metals and supported European currency pairs.** The Dollar Index dropped 0.87% over the week as markets digested mixed Federal Reserve rate signals and monitored impending US sanctions on Iran. Weakness in the greenback pushed EUR/USD up 1.32% toward three-month highs. The softer dollar and geopolitical uncertainty also fuelled a 5.56% rally in gold, which provided relative support to commodity-heavy equity benchmarks, allowing the FTSE 100 to gain 0.62%.

## Why It Matters for Next Week

1. **Nvidia’s upcoming Q2 earnings report will test global tech valuations and AI expenditure narratives.** With Nvidia scheduled to report Q2 earnings on August 26 against expected revenue of $91 billion, markets will scrutinize whether corporate earnings justify the expanding capital expenditures across the sector. A failure to exceed high market expectations could trigger further downside across global supply chains, directly impacting European technology suppliers like ASML.

2. **Developments surrounding US sanctions on Iran will dictate short-term energy and inflation trajectories.** Investors are closely tracking potential policy shifts following reports of 'Operation Economic Fury' aimed at Iran. Sustained upward momentum in Brent crude would reinforce headline inflation risks across the euro area, challenging current central bank expectations and potentially forcing the ECB into a more hawkish stance.

3. **US PCE inflation data and Federal Reserve signals will clarify rate cut timing.** Economic releases scheduled for August 24–28, including PCE inflation figures and official Fed communications, will provide critical direction for fixed-income markets. Upside surprises in US inflation data would likely sustain the upward pressure on 2-year and 10-year Treasury yields, maintaining downside pressure on equity valuations.

4. **Euro area rate volatility and exchange rate strength present positioning risks.** Despite euro area 10-year sovereign yields rising alongside regional inflation fears, EUR/USD advanced 1.32% over the week while the Dollar Index fell 0.87%. If upcoming economic releases confirm economic stagnation across the euro area rather than persistent demand-driven inflation, the euro's recent strength against the dollar may face repricing.
