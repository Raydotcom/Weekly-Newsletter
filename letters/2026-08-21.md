# Weekly Market Letter
## Week of 17-21 August 2026

## 1. What Moved Markets This Week

1. **Geopolitical tensions surrounding Iran pushed Brent crude up 6.63% and reignited euro area inflation fears, weighing on European equities after early-week highs.** Reports pointed to US policy actions—including the launch of "Operation Economic Fury" and discussions of tougher sanctions—as the primary trigger for rising energy prices. Higher energy costs lifted Euro area 10-year sovereign yields by 6 to 8 basis points, causing benchmark indices like the Euro Stoxx 50 (-1.18%) and DAX (-1.15%) to retreat from record highs set earlier in the week.

2. **Growing scrutiny over massive corporate expenditure on artificial intelligence weighed heavily on technology mega-caps and European semiconductor suppliers.** Headlines highlighted rising debt levels and structural profitability challenges tied to an estimated $3 trillion in AI capital spending. These concerns led to notable sell-offs in hardware and infrastructure providers ahead of Nvidia's upcoming earnings report, with Meta dropping 6.77%, Nvidia falling 4.64%, and ASML slipping 4.66%.

3. **Divergent inflation indicators left European rate expectations caught between supply-side energy shocks and moderating domestic wage pressures.** While energy price spikes drove euro zone bond yields higher and prompted traders to price in a more hawkish ECB stance, internal ECB reporting and remarks from policymaker Olli Rehn stressed that wage growth across the euro zone remains moderate without secondary effects. Furthermore, the ECB’s Consumer Expectations Survey indicated that July inflation expectations among consumers had edged lower, creating a complex backdrop for monetary policy.

## 3. Why It Matters for Next Week

1. **Nvidia's upcoming earnings present a critical test for tech sentiment and AI capex sustainability.** With headlines highlighting $3 trillion in hidden debt and capital expenditure burdens across Big Tech, Nvidia's results are positioned as the key market catalyst. A strong report validating hardware demand would likely stabilize semiconductor names like ASML, whereas a disappointment would confirm structural spending concerns and deepen the tech pullback.

2. **Energy price transmission to European inflation expectations requires close monitoring.** Brent crude's surge to $94.39 poses a direct threat to euro zone disinflation. While the ECB Consumer Expectations Survey for July showed inflation expectations edging down, a sustained oil rally will force fixed income markets to price in a more hawkish ECB, placing further upward pressure on euro area 10-year yields.

3. **Divergence between market-cap and equal-weighted indexes points to market rotation.** The S&P 500 Equal Weight index trades at 97% of its 52-week range, significantly outperforming the market-cap-weighted S&P 500 (-1.43%) and Nasdaq 100 (-2.45%). This positioning data suggests underlying market breadth remains intact despite mega-cap tech liquidation. A stabilization in tech would re-establish broader index momentum, whereas further tech selling risks pulling equal-weighted benchmarks off their range highs.

4. **Gold's technical breakout against a weakening US dollar signals heightened geopolitical hedging.** Gold's 5.56% advance cleared its 200-day moving average at the same time the Dollar Index fell 0.87% to 98.80. In the absence of a clear macroeconomic release driving the move, this price action reflects structural positioning for geopolitical risk linked to the Middle East. Continued escalation will keep upward pressure on commodities and safe-haven assets, while any diplomatic easing would likely trigger rapid unwinding.
