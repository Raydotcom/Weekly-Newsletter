# Weekly Market Letter
## Week of 17-21 August 2026

## 1. What Moved Markets This Week

1. **Geopolitical tensions and rising energy prices pushed European bond yields higher while weighing on equity sentiment.** Headlines indicated that US sanctions on Iran and escalating Middle Eastern conflict sparked fresh inflation concerns across the euro area. Brent crude spiked 6.63%, driving Euro area 10-year yields up (all-issuer yields rose 7.5 bps) as traders braced for a potentially more hawkish European Central Bank response. Major European benchmark indices ended the week in the red, with the Euro Stoxx 50 falling 1.18% as energy-driven rate pressures offset earlier gains.

2. **Euro area monetary policy expectations shifted amid conflicting inflation signals and official ECB communications.** Headlines highlighted growing trader expectations for a hawkish ECB stance in response to energy-driven headline risks. However, primary ECB communications provided a more temperate perspective: July survey data showed consumer inflation expectations edging down, while ECB board member Piero Cipollone stated that eurozone stagnation and severe inflation surges remain "rather remote." Moderate wage growth reports further suggested that second-round inflationary effects have not materialized.

3. **Tech valuations and AI capital expenditure came under scrutiny ahead of key earnings, dragging heavily on global growth stocks.** Reports highlighted growing market concern over $3 trillion in hidden off-balance-sheet debt and elevated capital expenditures tied to mega-cap technology AI investments. Ahead of its scheduled August 26 earnings release, NVIDIA fell 4.64%, while European semiconductor leader ASML dropped 4.66%. The broader tech retrenchment weighed heavily on the Nasdaq 100 (-2.45%) and Meta (-6.77%).

## 3. Why It Matters for Next Week

1. **NVIDIA's second-quarter earnings release on August 26 will test global technology valuations and AI investment sentiment.** With consensus expectations pointing to $91 billion in revenue, the report serves as a major test after weeks of market focus on tech sector leverage and high capital expenditures. Given reports that the stock has dropped after five of its last six earnings releases, a simple revenue beat may not be enough to restore momentum to global tech names like ASML.

2. **Energy market volatility remains the primary transmission mechanism for European rate expectations.** The 6.63% surge in Brent crude directly drove euro area 10-year sovereign yields higher as markets priced in energy-driven inflation risks. Continued upward pressure on oil futures following US sanctions on Iran would reinforce hawkish pricing for the ECB, keeping pressure on European fixed income and growth equities.

3. **Divergence between market pricing and ECB guidance will face scrutiny during upcoming economic data releases.** While market headlines indicated traders are bracing for a hawkish ECB, official statements from board members emphasized that inflation surges remain unlikely and wage growth stays moderate. Scheduled economic reporting for August 24–28 will provide crucial confirmation on whether underlying euro area inflation justifies hawkish market pricing.

4. **Equity market breadth indicators suggest resilient underlying positioning despite megacap drag.** With the S&P 500 Equal Weight standing near the top of its 52-week range while cap-weighted technology benchmarks pull back, broader equity market participation remains firm. A resolution of tech earnings uncertainty could allow broader European and US indexes to re-engage their primary uptrends.
